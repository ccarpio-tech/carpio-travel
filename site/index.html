Vietnam

I traveled Vietnam from north to south, from the mountains to the cities and eventually down to the beaches. Every stop felt completely different, but the people, the food, and the atmosphere made the whole country special to me. These are some of the places and moments that stood out along the way.

Hanoi

Hanoi was my introduction to Vietnam. I loved the narrow streets, the energy, St. Joseph’s Cathedral, and especially the food. I had phở from a Michelin-starred restaurant, tried egg coffee that tasted almost like tiramisu, and somehow even ended up loving the little donuts sold on the street.

Sapa

A mountain town in the far northwest, often wrapped in mist, surrounded by rice terraces and villages like Cat Cat.

Ha Giang Loop

A multi-day motorbike loop through the mountains near the Chinese border, on switchback roads like the one in this site's background.

Ninh Binh

Limestone karsts rising out of rice fields and rivers, often called “Ha Long Bay on land.” Best seen from the top of Hang Mua or from a rowboat on the river, followed by the local specialty: mountain goat.

Hue

Vietnam's imperial capital under the Nguyen dynasty, on the Perfume River: the Citadel, Truong Tien Bridge, and the giant dragon of the abandoned Thuy Tien water park outside the city.

Hoi An & Da Nang

Hoi An's lantern-lit old town is a UNESCO World Heritage Site about 30 km from the beach city of Da Nang, known for its street games at night, cooking and coffee classes, and some of the best food in central Vietnam.

Da Lat

A cool highland city in the Central Highlands, known for pine forests, flowers, coffee, and bánh tráng nướng, often called “Vietnamese pizza.”

Ho Chi Minh City

Vietnam's largest city, formerly Saigon: the French colonial-era Central Post Office, bridges lit up at night, Bui Vien walking street, and broken rice on every corner.

Phu Quoc

An island off Vietnam's southwest coast with a colorful European-style town, a clock tower that lights up at night, cozy cafes, and fresh seafood.

Laos

Laos was the slowest and most peaceful part of my trip. I crossed the border from Cambodia to Don Det in the 4,000 Islands, made my way north to Luang Prabang for its gilded temples, Mekong sunsets, and the turquoise pools of Kuang Si Falls, and finished in Vang Vieng, where hot-air balloons rise over the mountains at sunrise.

Luang Prabang

Luang Prabang was one of my stops in northern Laos. Temples, night markets, the Mekong River, and a much slower atmosphere than the larger cities I visited.

Vang Vieng

Vang Vieng had some of the most dramatic scenery of the trip. Limestone mountains surround the town, and seeing hot-air balloons above them at sunrise was incredible.

Don Det

Don Det sits in the 4,000 Islands region of southern Laos along the Mekong River. This part of the trip was much more relaxed and slower paced.

Cambodia

From Phuket I flew to Cambodia. In Siem Reap I explored the temples of Angkor, including Angkor Wat and Ta Prohm, and spent the evenings on Pub Street and at the night markets. Then I traveled south to the capital, Phnom Penh, before crossing the border into Laos.

Siem Reap

The gateway town for the temples of Angkor. I visited Angkor Wat, the centerpiece of the Khmer Empire and one of the largest religious monuments in the world, and Ta Prohm, where giant tree roots grow straight through the stone. After the temples: Pub Street and the night markets.

Phnom Penh

Cambodia's capital, where the Mekong and Tonlé Sap rivers meet: the Royal Palace by day and neon-lit streets after dark.

Thailand

Thailand was where my trip began. I started in Bangkok with Wat Arun, golden Buddhas, and late-night tuk-tuk rides, headed north to Chiang Mai for its temples, elephants, and khao soi, then finished on Phuket with beach sunsets, night markets, and plenty of street food.

Bangkok

Temples like Wat Arun on the Chao Phraya River, golden Buddhas, river boats, and late-night tuk-tuk rides.

Chiang Mai

Northern Thailand's cultural center: the ruined chedi of Wat Chedi Luang, elephants, and the region's signature curry noodle soup, khao soi.

Phuket

Thailand's largest island, on the Andaman Sea: beach sunsets, Naka Weekend Market, light displays at the night markets, and street food like crocodile skewers and crispy pork.
